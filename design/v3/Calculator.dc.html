Method

How the calculator works

In both models, payback is the first day revenue per install reaches the cost per install.

Subscription apps

  1. 01Payers per install = install-to-trial rate × trial-to-paid rate.
  2. 02Each payment is the plan price after store commission and refunds.
  3. 03The first charge lands when the trial ends, then one charge per billing cycle (7 days weekly, 30 monthly, 365 annual).
  4. 04At every renewal the same share of payers stays (renewal retention), so payments shrink cycle by cycle.
  5. 05Revenue per install on any day = payers per install × net payment × payments reached so far.

Ad-monetised apps

  1. 01A retention curve is drawn through your day 1 and day 30 retention (a power curve, so it falls fast and then flattens).
  2. 02Active days per install = the install day plus the retained share of every day after it.
  3. 03Revenue per install = revenue per active user per day × active days.

The search runs to day 3,650; when it finds no crossing, the calculator says so rather than calling it never.

Why your dashboard can show a later payback

The calculator models the revenue a cohort really earns. Your MMP and ad platforms often see less of it. Three switches under the chart simulate the usual gaps. With a gap switched on, the reported line falls below the real one, and campaigns that pay back look as if they do not.

Closing those gaps is the work behind the attribution audit and subscription revenue tracking.

Limits

Limits of the model

There is no account data here and no fitted cohort history. It produces a modelled outcome under those assumptions, not a forecast.

Check it against your own data
01Geometric per-cycle retention — a constant renewal rate every billing cycle.
02Constant ARPDAU across tenure.
03No seasonality in eCPM.
04No organic uplift from paid spend.
05No involuntary-churn recovery (failed payments never retried).
06Steady-state approximation — no cohort-size or price changes over the horizon.
07Surplus is before operating costs, so it is not profit.
