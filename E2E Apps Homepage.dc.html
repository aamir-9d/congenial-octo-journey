WHY YOUR NUMBERS DON'T RECONCILE

Three places the money goes missing. And one place it was never there.

The first isn't a measurement failure, and no amount of attribution accuracy will save you from it. The other three are — the revenue exists and you can't see it.

01 · NOT A MEASUREMENT PROBLEM

Retention nobody watches past D7.

Every LTV model is retention multiplied by monetisation. If the retention curve dies before the payback horizon, the campaign cannot break even at any CPI — the money was never going to be there. Most teams track D1, quote D7 in a deck, and never build the D30 curve that actually decides it. On a subscription app, payback lands somewhere around month nine. A cohort that's gone by week three never gets there.

d1 0.32 → d7 0.11 → d30 0.03 · curve dies before payback
02

Renewals nobody counted.

Subscription renewals happen on Apple's servers while your app is closed. If nothing is listening server-side, that revenue never reaches your MMP or your ad platforms — so every channel looks worse than it is, and you optimise against your own best cohorts.

revenue_dashboard ≠ mmp_reported · gap grows monthly
03

A conversion schema built for nothing in particular.

SKAdNetwork gives you a handful of bits and one short window. Most schemas were set up once, copied from a blog post, and never mapped to the events that actually predict revenue for this app.

skan_cv: null · 71% of postbacks
04

The web-to-app gap.

Someone clicks a Google ad on your website, installs later, subscribes a week after that. Without gclid/gbraid/wbraid capture and a conversion sent back, Google never learns which click earned the money — so it optimises toward the wrong people.

web_click → install → ??? → subscription

Three of these are fixable in weeks. The first is a product problem, and we'll tell you if that's the one you have.

HOW THE WORK IS SEQUENCED

The Full Loop

Four stations. The output of the last one is the input to the first. That's the point.

STATION 01

Instrument

Get the raw signal right at the source. Event taxonomy and naming, a written tracking plan the developers can actually implement, SDK integration and QA, consent and ATT prompt sequencing, debug and sandbox validation before anything ships.

Outcome: Events fire when they should, named what they should be, carrying what they should carry.

STATION 02

Attribute

Connect the signal to a source. MMP configuration and audit, SKAdNetwork and AdAttributionKit conversion schemas, attribution window alignment, server-to-server events, deferred deep linking, conversion APIs to every ad platform, web-to-app click capture.

Outcome: Every install and every dollar traceable to the click that caused it.

STATION 03

Decide

Turn clean data into a spending decision. Cohort LTV and retention decay, CAC payback curves by channel and by country, trial-to-paid economics, breakeven ceilings per market, paywall funnel diagnosis.

Outcome: A defensible number for what you can afford to pay for a user — market by market.

STATION 04

Deploy

Spend against those numbers. Google Ads app and web-to-app campaigns, Apple Ads, Meta, TikTok, ASO and store listing strategy, paywall and pricing tests, ad monetisation and mediation.

Outcome: Budget deployed where the arithmetic says it works — and fresh signal flowing back into Station 01.

WHAT THE AUDITS TURN UP

Three findings, from real audits.

11% → 17%

A dashboard reported an 11% trial-to-paid rate. The verified figure was closer to 17%. Every budget decision made from that dashboard had been built on the wrong number.

~5%

Share of new users in one app who ever reached a payment screen. Nobody had measured it. That single number was the reason the app lost money.

Most of the budget

At one portfolio audit, the majority of spend was going to countries that mathematically could not break even. It had run that way for months.
